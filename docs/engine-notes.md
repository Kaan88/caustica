## Real lenses and glare (track 16, 2026-11-02)

- **Lens systems** (`src/lens.rs`, `camera_ray` in pathtrace.hlsl): `Settings::lens_system` (0 = the 5c thin lens; 1–4 `lens::LENSES`) applies to the perspective model only, and `Settings::real_lens()` makes `perspective()` false, so RR, the temporal upscalers, picking, the gizmo and BDPT light tracing are off. Prescriptions are pbrt / Kolb 1995 tables (radius, thickness, n_d, diameter; mm). `INFO` measures each lens: focal length from a paraxial ray from infinity (49.97, 21.78, 9.98, 50.66 mm) and the wide-open f-number from the entrance pupil (bisection on the height of an axial bundle: f/2.00, 2.75, 4.00, 2.81). The unit tests check these against the names.
- **`lens::pack`** (rebuilt by `Gpu::prepare_lens` when lens, f-number, focus, fov, aspect or dispersion change): stops down by scaling the stop radius by N₀/N; focuses with a paraxial ray from the axial object point (`focus_distance` × `lens_scale` × 1000 mm in front of the front vertex) and puts the film where it crosses the axis (clamped to 4 f); film half height = (f + focus extension) · tan(fov/2), or f · 1.6 · fov/2 for the fisheye (equidistant, so fov 100° shows its whole 156° circle). Exit-pupil bounds for 32 radial film bands (a 40² grid on the rear vertex plane, 4 film points per band, grown one cell; symmetric in y), and the centre's effective pupil E[pass · cos⁴θ] · area, which normalizes the exposure to 1 at the frame centre for every lens and f-number.
- **Shader:** film point = −ndc × film half size (the lens inverts the image), a uniform point in its band's bounds rotated to the film point's angle, `trace_lens` through every surface (pbrt's sphere-root choice; the stop is `inside_aperture`, so the blade count shapes the bokeh), then lens space (mm, −z forward) to world at `real_lens.z` units per mm with the eye at the front vertex. `camera_weight` = cos⁴θ · band area / centre pupil; vignetted samples are invalid (zero). The guide ray goes through the band's centre (falls back to the pinhole ray when that is blocked). BDPT and photon gathering multiply their camera throughput by `camera_weight` too.
- **Dispersion:** per-surface indices at 610 / 550 / 465 nm from a Cauchy fit through n_d and an Abbe number guessed from n_d (60 at 1.52 down to 28 at 1.80); `dispersion` scales the spread. With it on, each sample traces one random channel with weight 3. Off by default: the guessed glasses undo the designs' achromatic pairs and give rainbow fringes (purple fringing on the wide angle's light at 1).
- **Look** (`captures/lens/`, 640×400): framing and centre exposure match the pinhole; the Double Gauss at fov 60 (past its 44° field) shows mechanical and cos⁴ vignetting; the fisheye's barrel distortion; the singlet's spherical-aberration glow; fairy-lights at f/2 vs f/8 at scale 0.05 (bokeh and brightness).
- **Glare** (`Settings::glare`, `glare_size`, `glare_threshold`; display only, out of `transport_key`): `glare_pass` in post.hlsl, one dispatch of 512 groups (a group is a row or column, 256 threads, Stockham radix-2 FFT in groupshared memory for 3 channels) per mode, constants in slots 13+ (`CB_SLOTS` 23). When blades, rotation or size change (`glare_kernel`): the aperture (4×4 supersampled, `glare_settings.z` = 256 − 232 · size texels across) into region 0, row and column FFTs, then the PSF per channel |F(k/s)|²/s² with s = λ/550 nm, normalized by Parseval to unit sum, into region 1, and its FFT. Every frame: the highlights (luminance above the threshold, 4×4 box per cell of the 384-px image in the 512² grid; the padding keeps the circular convolution from wrapping), forward rows, columns forward × kernel × inverse, inverse rows with the 1/N². `lens_and_film` adds glare × strength (bilinear) before the vignette. A small bright light with 6 blades gives a 6-spike starburst with rainbow spikes; extended or dim sources give a coloured bloom.
- **Self-test:** 289 (Double Gauss at f/4, 6 blades, dispersion, glare) and 290 (fisheye) check that the image is finite and lit with RR off; 291 resets. `SCENES_FROM` is 292.

## BDPT and photon mapping (track 15 part 2, 2026-11-01)

- **Where:** `shaders/integrators.hlsl` (includes pathtrace.hlsl, compiled with `-D TEXTURE_MAPS`), `Settings::integrator` (0 path, 1 BDPT, 2 photon mapping), `Gpu::integrate` (one integrator over one region) and `Gpu::prepare_aux`. Both new integrators write the guides through `write_guides` (moved out of `trace`), use white noise and a box pixel filter, treat glass as solid in shadow rays (`glass_blocks`, so caustics come from real paths), and write a running mean into the accumulation like `trace` (`finish_frame`; the direct-light AOV holds the beauty). Not supported: volumes and fog, portals, nested media (a single glass, entered by the side of the hit), `mis_mode`, the LPE / path-record variants, more than 10 bounces.
- **aux** (root parameter 15, u3 space 2, a raw buffer): photon mapping's counter, 2^20 list heads and 32-byte photons (position, incoming direction, power, next) at 0, capacity photons × min(bounces, 10) (photons and the gather stop at 10 bounces; `photons` is at most 2^20, about 340 MB); BDPT's splats (rgb floats per render pixel) at `splat_offset`, zeroed by a copy when allocated and by `bdpt_resolve` after every frame.
- **BDPT** (PBRT-v3's formulation): a camera subpath and a light subpath of up to bounces + 2 / + 1 vertices in local arrays (`BVertex`: position, normal, throughput, area pdfs both ways, material and texture values), then every strategy (s, t) with s + t − 2 ≤ bounces, weighted by the balance heuristic over all of them (`mis_weight`, with the four endpoint densities passed in). Light subpaths start on table triangles and spheres (uniform area, cosine emission, both sides of a two-sided panel), chosen as next-event estimation chooses them, so s = 1 and the subpath share one origin density. The HDRI, the sky and the suns start no subpath: they keep the path tracer's two strategies (hits and NEE) with its MIS. Light tracing (t = 1) splats with float compare-exchange adds; it needs a pinhole perspective or tilt-shift camera without RR's jitter (`integrator.w` bit 0), otherwise the camera counts as a delta vertex. The pinhole importance uses the image-plane area times the region's share of the image (`photon.y`), which keeps camera and light strategies' sample counts consistent in battle mode. Dielectrics are delta vertices (η² only on camera subpaths); emission outside the light table (procedural emitters) is found by hits alone.
- **Visibility between subpaths** (`connected`): both ends move off their surfaces toward each other. With a shadow ray stopping a fixed 0.6 mm short of the far point (as NEE's do), grazing connections hit the far point's own surface (it lies a hair below a hit computed through an instance transform): the lit door of veach-ajar reached the wall at 35% of its light, the image 4% dark. A non-finite strategy term is dropped on its own (`add_term`), not the whole sample.
- **Photon mapping** (Knaus and Zwicker 2011, probabilistic progressive photon mapping): each frame `photon_clear` empties the grid, `photon_trace` emits `photons` × the region's share (sky and HDRI from a disk of the scene's bounding sphere, suns likewise, table emitters as BDPT's light subpaths), Russian roulette from the third bounce, deposits from the second hit on at gather surfaces (all but near-mirror metals, roughness < 0.2), into cells one radius wide (hashed, linked lists). `photon_gather` follows camera paths through delta surfaces and near mirrors to the first gather surface, takes direct light by NEE plus one BSDF ray (balance heuristic) and indirect light from the photons in the 27 neighbouring cells. The radius starts at `photon_radius` × the bounding radius (rebuild_scene; the floor is left out) and shrinks by (i + 2/3)/(i + 1) in r² per frame (`photon_frames`, `photon_scale`, reset with the accumulation), so frames are independent estimates and the running mean converges; the bias fades as samples grow.
- **Battle mode** (`Settings::battle`, kept across scene loads): quadrants path tracing, path + ReSTIR DI, BDPT and photon mapping, each dispatched over its region (`region` constant: trace, the ReSTIR passes, the new entries all offset and bound-check by it; ReSTIR's neighbours and history stay inside it) with its own constants (slots 9–12 × CB_STRIDE) and its own sample count (`Gpu::battle`). Timestamps 5–12 bracket each quadrant; the slowest one takes `spp` samples per frame and the others as many as fit in its time (at most 64, fractions carried). The photon quadrant takes one gather per frame and spends its share on photons instead (×1–4 its quarter of `photons`): gathers of one frame share a photon map, and counting them as samples ran its count to the cap 64 times sooner (found in review). RR, the native denoiser and the upscalers are off; `Gpu::samples` is the smallest quadrant's count. The overlay draws the split and each quadrant's samples and GPU seconds; with a reference loaded, the convergence graph plots each quadrant's RMSE×1000 against its GPU time.
- **Checked** (box filter for all, glass casting shadows, the same bounces; image mean against a path-traced reference): presets 0 and 3 BDPT 1.0003 / 1.0007 (per-pixel RMSE×1000 1.0 / 1.9 at 4096 spp), photon mapping 1.0003 / 1.0002; desert-monolith (sun) 1.0000 both; hdri-noon-spheres 0.998 / 0.997; cornell-caustic 1.003 both; veach-ajar BDPT 1.005 (the path tracer's own MIS and NEE-only renders differ by up to 1.6% in its dark half), photon mapping 1.03 at 16k spp (radius bias, falling). fairy-lights: both +1% over the path tracer, which is the light-sampling gap in roadmap "Known issues". Preset 3 needed 10 bounces (it sets 10): with BDPT capped at 8 its glass quadrant was 1.1% dark.
- **Equal samples on cornell-caustic** (160×90, RMSE×1000 against 65k spp): path 23.6, BDPT 6.8, photon mapping 5.1, at 1.2 / 8.2 / 3.5 ms per 16 spp: at equal time BDPT has about 1.8× and photon mapping about 7× less variance than path tracing there. On veach-ajar at equal time (about 220 ms): path 4.5, BDPT 5.9, photon mapping 6.1.
- **Cost** (release, 1440×900, preset 3, 4 spp, GPU ms per frame, RR / native): path tracing 9.6 / 10.3; BDPT 97 / 126 (the light-tracing splats run only without RR's jitter); photon mapping 6.6 / 6.0 (2^18 photons per frame); battle mode 48. With the integrator at path tracing nothing changed: presets 0 and 3, RR and native, two interleaved runs each against the ReSTIR snapshot, all within ±0.6 ms of noise. The scene bounds for sun and sky photons are measured only when photon mapping runs after a rebuild: measuring them in every `rebuild_scene` (all object vertices) made each timeline-export frame 10 ms slower and broke the self-test's export.
- **Native denoiser:** the new integrators keep the guide ray's albedo (coverage 1) and an empty specular AOV, so the native denoiser may fringe at edges and ghost on mirrors and glass with them; `trace` accumulates the samples' own albedo. RR is unaffected. Views 7 and 10 (error, variance) assume one sample count, so they are wrong across battle quadrants.
- **Self-test:** frames 285–287 (preset 3: BDPT, photon mapping, battle) check that the image is finite and lit and that every quadrant rendered.

## ReSTIR DI (track 15 part 1, 2026-11-01)

- **What it does:** with `Settings::restir` (Render → Advanced; off by default, on in `fairy-lights`), the light sample at a path's first hit comes from per-pixel reservoirs (Bitterli et al. 2020) instead of one draw from the power CDF. It covers the whole emitter table (triangles, sphere lights, suns); the HDRI keeps its own strategy, chosen with the same probability as before, and its MIS. Later bounces and volume scatters are unchanged.
- **Samples:** an emitter index plus two 16-bit numbers that name the same point from every surface: uniform area on a triangle or on the whole sphere (not its visible cap), a direction in a sun's cone. Shifting a sample between pixels or frames is the identity, with Jacobian 1. Source pdf = power selection × 1/area (sun: 1/(2π·om)).
- **Target:** unshadowed luminance of the table's flat emission × G × cos × a cheap BSDF (Lambert floored at 0.05/π, plus one isotropic GGX lobe), from per-surface terms read once (`make_domain`). A triangle point seen from behind gets 2% weight, not 0: on a closed emitter it is hidden by the object itself, which an unshadowed target cannot see. Measured on `fairy-lights`, direct light at 1 spp: flat-region RMSE 49 → 24 with the 2% weight. Keeping it above 0 keeps the result unbiased for two-sided panels and for a camera inside a luminous room.
- **Passes** (all in pathtrace.hlsl, compiled with the map code):
  - `restir_presample`: the frame's light tiles (Wyman and Panteleev 2021), 128 × 1024 samples drawn from the source distribution through Walker's alias table (`lighting::alias_records`, packed after the emitters in the emitter buffer, eight pairs per record; plain NEE keeps its CDF, so it is unchanged). An entry is stored in a `Reservoir` record: light | kind << 30, uv, source pdf, the emission's luminance, point, normal. Every 8×8 group takes its candidates from one tile, which keeps the reads in cache. Candidates that share a tile are correlated, but RIS only needs each one's marginal pdf, so this stays unbiased. On fairy-lights (150k emitter triangles, RR 1 spp, 1440×900), ReSTIR's overhead was +19.3 ms with a CDF search per candidate, +14.3 ms with the alias table and +2.5 ms with tiles, at the same quality.
  - `restir_initial` (z = layer): the guide ray's hit, `restir_candidates` RIS candidates from a tile (default 8), then a temporal merge with last frame's final reservoir at the reprojected pixel (normal > 0.8, within 5% of the view distance; history M capped at `restir_history`, default 20).
  - `restir_spatial` (z = layer): up to `restir_neighbors` (default 4) random neighbours within 30 px (at 1080 lines), normal > 0.9 and the plane distance within 5% of the view distance. Pairwise MIS (3k + 1 target evaluations instead of (k + 1)²).
  - `trace`: at bounce 0, sample s < layers merges its layer's final reservoir with one fresh candidate at the actual hit (the native pixel filter, depth of field and portals move the hit off the reservoir's surface). Weight 1, so BSDF hits on table emitters from that vertex count 0 (`previous_restir`), except a sphere light seen from inside it (`restir_covers`), which the area samples cannot reach (found in review).
- **Unbiased:** every merge is generalized RIS (Lin et al. 2022) with balance or pairwise weights that sum to 1 over the merged surfaces. Domains are picked by geometry only. A reservoir that holds no sample (W = 0) still takes part with its M: skipping it conditioned on its random outcome and measured +1.1% with one candidate. Visibility enters only through the final shadow ray (no visibility reuse). Checked at 4096 spp against light sampling only (`mis_mode` 1, 1 bounce): image mean within 0.1% and RMSE×1000 below that of an independent NEE run, on fairy-lights, candle-shelf and night-skyline. With all bounces, ReSTIR matches NEE-only on presets 0, light-temperature-row and colonnade-night (0.9999 vs 1.0002, 0.9979 vs 0.9976, 0.9995 vs 0.9994).
- **NEE vs MIS gap (pre-existing, not ReSTIR):** on night-skyline, light sampling only is 0.57% darker than MIS and BSDF only (which agree to 0.05%). light-temperature-row: −0.24%. ReSTIR inherits it at the first bounce. Not found: a distance-relative shadow-ray margin did not change it. See roadmap "Known issues".
- **Layers:** samples within a frame would all share one reservoir, so each of the frame's first `layers` samples has its own chain: `min(spp, 4)`, capped so the buffer stays ≤ 1 GiB (4K: 2 layers). Later samples use plain light sampling. Buffer: root UAV u2 space 2 (root parameter 14), 2 × layers × render pixels × 32 B (final reservoirs, then `restir_initial`'s), allocated while `restir` is on. The light tiles follow (4 MiB). A new buffer, a full reset (`reset(true)`) or any material or emitter rebuild (light painting skips the reset) drops the history; camera moves keep it.
- **Constants:** `restir` = flags (bit 0 on, bit 1 history valid, bits 8–15 layers), candidates, neighbours, history cap. The passes always use white noise (`white_noise`; `random()` is unchanged in `trace`).
- **Gain** (480×270, native raw, the end of a 1-spp `--orbit-test` against a 4096-spp reference, RMSE×1000 of l/(1+l) on flat pixels (93–98% of the image), direct light only): fairy-lights 62.3 → 24.0 (6.7× less variance), candle-shelf 42.4 → 12.8 (11×). With all bounces the gain is smaller, since indirect light dominates a white box: fairy-lights whole-image 97.5 → 88.8 before the back-face weight.
- **Cost** (1440×900, release, GPU ms, one run each, off → on): fairy-lights RR 1 spp 14.1 → 17.6, RR 4 spp 41.3 → 56.3, native 1 spp 11.6 → 14.2; candle-shelf 6.3 → 7.2, 12.7 → 18.9, 3.1 → 4.7; preset 0 4.0 → 4.4, 6.1 → 8.5, 1.3 → 1.8. That is roughly +0.5–3.5 ms per layer: the guide ray in `restir_initial`, 8 candidates, the spatial pass and the merge in `trace`. Off, nothing runs: the other shaders compile to the same code paths (`white_noise` is false, `previous_restir` stays false).
- **Self-test:** frames 283–284 (every light kind, then the sun alone) run with ReSTIR and 2 layers and check that the passes ran. `fairy-lights` runs it again in the scene sweep.
- **Not done:** visibility reuse (a shadow ray in the initial pass; biased unless the spatial MIS weights trace rays too); ReSTIR at later bounces (ReSTIR GI / PT); emissive texture maps and procedural emitters are still outside the table; the target ignores coat, sheen, anisotropy and transmission; the temporal history is dropped on any non-camera change, including every animation frame; the spatial radius is fixed, not adaptive; with a static camera and accumulation, temporal reuse correlates frames, so it converges more slowly than plain sampling (turn it off for offline renders, or set History to 0); no debug view of reservoir M or W.

## Light path expressions (track 14 part 2, 2026-11-01)

- **Language** (`lpe.rs`): OSL-style regular expressions over D G S T V (scattering) and L B (the light event), `.`, `R` = [DGS], sets, `[^ ]`, `( )`, `|`, `* + ?`; an optional leading C. Parsed to a Thompson NFA, subset-constructed, minimised (Moore) with states that can never accept dropped; at most 32 states (subset construction stops at 4096 subsets, so ".* D . . . L" fails fast); an expression whose language has no string ending in L or B is rejected. A leading C is stripped once, before any `|`. `Settings::lpe` (≤ 4, validated; kept across scene and preset loads) and `lpe_slot` (display only, excluded from the transport key). The table (t8, root parameter 13, an upload buffer): per slot, state and event, next | accept(next) << 8, 255 dead.
- **Events:** D, G (GGX, coat, sheen, grating orders; S instead below base roughness 0.1), S (a dielectric's reflection), T (refraction, any roughness), V (a volume scatter); L (emitters, suns, by NEE or by a hit), B (sky gradient, HDRI, physical sky: misses and HDRI NEE). Portals and nested-dielectric pass-throughs are no events.
- **Shader:** the `trace_lpe` variant (`-D TEXTURE_MAPS -D LPE`, picked while any slot is set; the other variants compile to the same code as before). Per path: the DFA states (8 bits per slot in one uint) and a label weight. A surface's light sample is split exactly into diffuse and the rest by `lpe_diffuse` (eval_bsdf's diffuse term after the sheen and coat dimming, times 1 − transmission). A continued path takes one label, drawn by a hash (not `random()`, so the beauty's samples are unchanged) with the luminance share of the diffuse part at the sampled direction, and the weight is multiplied by share / probability per channel: each slot is unbiased, a partition of all paths sums to the beauty in expectation. Sums per pixel in registers, then the running mean in `lpe_aov` (u1 space 2, root parameter 12, render pixels × slots float4, allocated while slots are set; a new buffer restarts the accumulation). Unclamped (the firefly clamp is ignored); non-finite contributions are dropped. Cost with 4 slots (preset 0, 1440×900, release): RR 5.33 → 6.90 ms, native 5.14 → 7.38 ms; off, the other variants are unchanged (5.31 / 5.15 before).
- **Display:** view 12 shows slot `lpe.y`, tone-mapped, and writes it to `linear_output` instead of the beauty, so `--capture x.exr` saves the AOV.
- **Checked:** the partition `C [LB]`, `C D .* [LB]`, `C [GS] .* [LB]`, `C [TV] .* [LB]` sums to the raw beauty (image mean to 1e-6; per-pixel RMSE×1000 of l/(1+l) 0.007–0.21 at 512–1024 spp) on presets 3 and 9, `scattering-materials` and `hdri-fire-sky-dragon`. `C D L` under MIS (labels drawn on BSDF hits) matches light sampling only (exact split) to 0.02% (preset 9) and 0.002% (3); `C [GS] L` within its noise (1.8% and 4.3%, both under 1σ: light sampling alone is very noisy on near-mirror lobes). Self-test 232–236 checks the partition sum (3%) at the self-test's sample count.
- **Not done:** object or material labels (OSL's `'name'` tags, "light off the red wall"); separate R / T for glossy transmission (rough glass is T); one AOV at a time on screen, no multi-layer EXR with every slot; AOVs are not denoised (RR / the native denoiser see only the beauty); 4 slots; light samples through portals don't exist, so portal paths are BSDF-found only; LPE costs the `trace_maps` code even in untextured scenes while on; exports clear the slots (the AOVs are not exported).

## Path visualizer (track 14 part 1, 2026-11-01)

- **Shader:** `trace` compiled a third time with `-D TEXTURE_MAPS -D PATH_RECORD` (`trace_paths` in build.rs and hotreload.rs). `recording` is a static const, and `record()` is empty without the define, so `trace` / `trace_maps` compile to the same code as before (only phi operand order differed in the disassembly). The recording variant runs on thread (0, 0) only, at render pixel `path_record.xy`, for `path_record.z` paths with the frame's sequence indices and RNG state: the first `sampling.y` are exactly the paths that frame accumulates, the rest the next indices. It writes no texture (the guide ray only sets `guide.material`).
- **Records** (`paths::Record`, 48 B: position, info = kind | bounce << 8 | path << 16, color, value, direction): the kinds and end reasons are listed above `trace`. Hit vertices are written after the next direction is chosen (kind = the lobe taken) with the throughput arriving and the luminance the hit added (emission, or the environment for an escape); light samples are written before their vertex (from, direction, distance, contribution, blocked); one end record per path (radiance after the firefly clamp, reason). Record 0 holds the count; capacity `PATH_CAPACITY` 16384.
- **GPU:** root parameter 11 is a root UAV (u0 space 2), bound in `bind()` for every dispatch. `Gpu::path_request` (output pixel, count; mapped to the render pixel as the light meter's) makes `render` dispatch one group before the frame's trace, copy the buffer to `path_readback`, and map it after Present into `path_result`.
- **App:** `UiState::paths` (`paths::PathView`). A left click while the window is open, or Alt+click, records instead of picking; P toggles the window; `--ui-paths X,Y[,I]` records (and selects path I) for UI captures. Lines are drawn with `View::segment` (clipped at the near plane), perspective camera only; escapes and sun / HDRI light samples are drawn `Settings::distance` long. Self-test frame 230 records 8 paths at the centre and checks their structure.
- **Not done:** LPEs (part 2); an animated photon along a path; NEE through portals and the false-hit pass-throughs of the shadow rays are not shown; the native filter's or RR's view of the pixel is not shown (the paths are raw samples); no export of the paths (OBJ / JSON); 64 paths at high bounce counts can fill the 16384 records (later paths are dropped, the window says so); the recording dispatch sits inside the frame's trace timestamp on the frame of a click.

## Procedural surfaces (track 13, 2026-11-01)

- **Objects:** `Object::sdf` (`Sdf`: none, exact sphere, exact torus, Mandelbulb, Menger level 5) renders the object as a procedural surface in the unit cube [−1, 1]³ of object space, under the usual transform. `kind()` returns a proxy mesh (sphere, torus, box) for CPU picking, framing and grounding; `geometry()` skips such objects (no GPU triangles, so the self-test counts still match); they are not in the emitter table (emission is found by BSDF hits only). The Scene tab's *Surface* combo picks it.
- **GPU:** one shared BLAS of one AABB (the unit cube), instanced per visible procedural object with `InstanceRecord.vertex_base` = the kind id; glass / hidden ones get FORCE_NON_OPAQUE like meshes. Every RayQuery loop (`intersect`, `occluded`, `surface_distance`) handles `CANDIDATE_PROCEDURAL_PRIMITIVE`: `sdf_hit` on the object ray (unnormalized, so t is the world t) and `CommitProceduralPrimitiveHit`; the glass-shadow and hidden-from-camera rules match triangles. A committed procedural hit takes its normal from the SDF gradient (tetrahedral differences), to world by the inverse transpose.
- **Intersection:** the sphere is analytic; the rest are sphere traced (128 steps, hit at 2e-4 object units; round shapes start at their bounding sphere; starting inside, for refraction, marches to the exit). The Mandelbulb uses Quilez's trigonometry-free power 8 (6 iterations; 3× cheaper than the polar form), the Menger sponge Quilez's cross subtraction.
- **Cost** (1440×900, native, `sdf-gallery` objects alone, GPU ms): mesh versions 5.6, exact sphere 4.9, exact torus 5.0, Mandelbulb 12.3, Menger 7.8; the gallery 19.5 (65 before the bounding sphere, the cheaper bulb and the step cap). Non-procedural scenes render the same as before (≤1e-6).
- **Scene:** `sdf-gallery`.

## Volumes (track 12, 2026-11-01)

- **Media.** Homogeneous only. An object's medium rides the nested-dielectric stack: a transmissive material with `density` > 0 (extinction per unit), `scatter_color` (single-scattering albedo) and `phase` (Henyey-Greenstein g). The colour still absorbs by Beer–Lambert as before. An IOR of 1 with roughness 0 makes an invisible boundary (smoke in a box). Global fog (`Settings::fog`) fills a box from the floor (y = −1) to `height`, ±`extent` on x and z, and exists only where the stack is empty (not inside glass).
- **Transport** (`pathtrace.hlsl`, after the false-hit loop): free-flight sampling on the grey extinction, so the pdf cancels the transmittance; a scatter before the surface multiplies by the albedo, does NEE through `sample_light` (shared with surfaces) weighted by the phase pdf with MIS, then samples HG. `previous_pdf` = phase pdf, so emission hits after a scatter are MIS-weighted like after a surface.
- **Shadow rays** (`medium_transmittance`): through a scattering object's medium up to the nearest surface (`surface_distance`, a minimal closest-hit query; refraction there ignored, as glass shadows do), then the fog past that point. Surface NEE applies it only when fog is on or the path is in a scattering medium. Clear glass keeps its old behaviour.
- **Packing:** `PackedMaterial` grew to 176 B (`medium` = albedo rgb + density, zeroed unless transmissive; `phase.x` = g). `Constants` gained `fog` (density, g, height, extent) and `fog_color`.
- **Cost:** a per-path `density` (the stack top's, updated with `absorption`) gates the volume code, so scenes without media pay ~1–3% GPU time (preset 0: 4.29 → ~4.35 ms; glass preset 3 within noise). Before that gate glass scenes paid 19%.
- **Checked:** non-volume renders match the previous build to 5e-7; Cornell box in fog, 32k spp: MIS vs NEE-only differ by 0.06% ± 0.13%.
- **Bounce budget:** a scatter uses one of the path's bounces, so dense media darken at the default 6 bounces; raise `bounces` in such scenes (the volume scenes use 8–32).
- **Absorption after false hits** (fixed with this track): the false-hit loop changed the medium stack without refreshing `absorption`, so leaving a higher-priority glass wall into water kept the glass's absorption; it now refreshes it (nested-dielectric scenes change slightly).
- **Denoisers:** RR handles haze well (cornell-fog, stage-beams). The native denoiser leaves the fog speckled: its demodulation and stops are surface-based.
- **Scenes:** `cornell-fog`, `stage-beams` (multiple scattering into the beams shows as sparse speckles at low spp: real variance), `scattering-materials` (milk, jade, smoke, wax, syrup), `underwater`.

## Upscalers (2026-10-29)

- **Setting:** `Settings.upscaler` (`Upscaler`: dlss default, fsr, xess, bicubic, bilinear). Used only when RR is off and `rw < width`.

  - `release_upscalers()` runs on resize and reconfigure.
- **Per-frame mode:** with a temporal upscaler active, `render()` calls `reset(false)` every drawn frame, so each frame is fresh jittered samples, as with RR. ## Analytic lights (track 7, 2026-10-30)

- **Kinds** (`scene::LightKind` on `AreaLight::kind`, default `rect`): `disk` (ellipse inscribed in `size`), `sphere` (diameter `size[0]`), `spot` (a disk plus `spread`, the full cone angle, and `blend`, the fading share of it), `sun` (`angle`, angular diameter; position only places its handle). The main light stays a rectangle. `AreaLight::direction()` is the rotated −Y; `radiance()` turns intensity into radiance: a rectangle's intensity is its radiance, a disk / spot / sphere's is divided by its projected area (π·a·b/4), so it shines as a 1 × 1 panel at any size, and a sun's by sin² of its angular radius (a white surface facing it reads the intensity). `cone()` gives the spot's outer and inner cosines (inner kept ≥ outer + 1e-5 for smoothstep).
- **Geometry** (`geometry::static_geometry`): a disk / spot is a 64-triangle fan (`DISK_SEGMENTS`, wound like the rectangle so cross(e1, e2) is the facing normal), a sphere the unit sphere mesh (64 × 32, vertices on the sphere, so the mesh is inscribed) scaled by the radius, a sun nothing. `geometry_key` includes the kind.
- **Materials** (`lighting::materials`): emission = `radiance()`; a sphere is two-sided (`optical.w` 0); a spot sets flag bit 5 (32) in `lobes.w` with the cosines in `secondary.xy`.
- **Emitter table** (`lighting::analytic`, `emitter_table(vertices, materials, analytic)`): suns first, then spheres, then triangles. Analytic entries have `position_area.w` −2 (sun: unit direction towards it in xyz, 1 − cos of its angular radius in `edge1_cdf.x`, computed as 2·sin²(α/2) for precision) or −1 (sphere: centre, radius). Lights of zero power get no entry (and a sphere's triangles then none either); `lighting::sun_count(&emitters)` counts the leading suns. Every triangle of a sphere's mesh maps (`emitter_lookup`) to the sphere's entry, so BSDF hits on the mesh get the sphere's pdf. Powers: sphere 4πr²·lum, spot triangles area·lum·½(sin²outer + sin²inner) (∫cos over the cone / π), suns lum·π·sin²α × a nominal 50-unit area. `Gpu::sun_count` goes to the constants as `lights.x` (the old unused `light_position_size`).
- **Shader:** `cone_sample` (uniform in the cone's solid angle, pdf 1/(2π·om); it draws 1 − cos and takes the sine from it, since a float cosine near 1 has too few steps for a 0.05° sun), `sphere_om` (1 − cos of the half angle a sphere covers, written s²/(1 + √(1 − s²)) so small far spheres don't cancel; 0 from inside), `spot()` (smoothstep on the cosine to the light's normal, on hits and in NEE). NEE on a sphere aims a shadow ray at the true sphere's near surface along the sampled direction; the inscribed mesh lies beyond it, so it never self-shadows. Misses test the first `lights.x` emitters (suns: inside when the direction's sin² to the sun, from a cross product, is below om·(2 − om)) with MIS against `(1 − p_env)·prob/(2π·om)`; hidden suns only from bounce 1. `emitter_pdf` handles spheres.
- **UI:** *Kind* combo, Diameter / Angle / Size by kind, Spread and Blend for spots, rotation in the main grid for spots and suns. *+ Light* is a menu of the kinds (`editor::new_light`: a bulb inside the Cornell volume, a spot above the stage, a sun from 35° / −30° at intensity 1). Gizmo outlines (`gizmo::light_outlines`) are polygons: rectangles, 32-point ellipses, a view-facing circle for spheres, a small disk for suns, plus a stroke along the direction for spots and suns; `polygon_hit` picks any of them. Scaling a sphere scales its diameter on every handle.
- **Checked:** MIS vs NEE-only vs BSDF-only means (480×300, raw native, open floor, sky 0): sphere 1.0000 / 1.0000, disk 0.9999 / 0.9998, spot 1.0000 / 1.0006 (4096 spp), sun 1.0000 / 0.9986 at 20° (block differences +0.15% ± 0.14%, noise); a 3° sun's BSDF-only image is 0.5% low at 4096 spp and a 1° sun's 5% low at 2048 but 0.14% at 8192: the heavy tail of hitting a small source. RR renders of a bulb and a spot in the Cornell box and a sun on the open floor look right.

## Video export (item 6, 2026-10-30)

- **Output size apart from the window:** `Gpu::width/height` is the output size, `swap_size` the swapchain's. `resize` = `resize_swap` (buffers, RTVs, `ui_frame`) + `resize_output` (upscalers released, all UAV textures). A UI capture (`ui_frame`) uses `swap_size`. `set_hdr` resizes to `swap_size`.
- **`video.rs`:** `Options` (size, fps, frames, spp, `seconds_per_frame` limit, `Format` H.264 / H.265 / HDR10 HEVC / ProRes / PNG / EXR sequence, `quality` = CRF or ProRes profile, orbit `degrees` / `pitch` / `distance` / `Easing`, path). ## Textures (core track 5, 2026-10-30)

- **Mesh maps** (`mesh.rs`): parts keep UVs (`Part::uv`, parallel to the mesh; glTF TEXCOORD_0, OBJ `vt` with v flipped) and `Maps { base, normal, metal_rough, cutoff }` of `Arc<Map>`. `Map` is RGBA8 plus a box-filtered mip chain down to 1×1 and per-channel averages (sRGB decoded for colour maps); maps over 8192 are halved. glTF images are decoded once per (image, colour space) on all cores (`decode_all`), 8/16-bit layouts (16-bit keeps the high byte); OBJ reads `map_Kd` and `norm`/`bump` with the `image` crate (jpeg feature added). A normal map must average near (0.5, 0.5, >0.6) (`looks_like_normals`), so grey OBJ height maps are ignored. glTF alpha MASK uses its cutoff, BLEND is treated as MASK at 0.5; the cutoff is divided by the base factor's alpha. `mesh::maps(o)` returns a part's maps when `o.material.textures` (a whole multi-part file has none); `has_maps` ignores the flag.
- **Values scale the maps:** the importer keeps colour = factor × average (as before), and the GPU multiplies each map by value / average (`base_scale`, `surface_scale`). So an imported mesh looks exactly as authored, a slider edit tints or shifts the map, and *Texture maps* off (`Material::textures`, default true, also in `geometry_key` for opacity) shows the averages, which is what older builds rendered. Old scene files therefore get their textures without changes.
- **Texture sets** (`texture.rs`): `Material::texture` (a path, ≤ 1024 chars) and `texture_scale` (repeats per unit, 0.01–100, default 0.5). The path is an image or any map of a set; the set is every image with the same prefix (`prefix`: the name up to the first map word), recognised by name: Color / BaseColor / Albedo / diff (base), NormalGL / nor_gl (normal), NormalDX / nor_dx (green flipped), Roughness, Metalness, and Poly Haven's ARM (G roughness, B metal, used as is). Roughness and metalness are packed into one map (G, B). Cached per path, failures too. *Load…* in the material editor sets colour, roughness and metal to the averages (`apply_averages`). A set wins over a mesh's own maps; walls (records 0–2) and floor (3) take sets too, and then use their own material colour instead of left / right / base (`lighting::materials`).
- **GPU** (`Gpu::attach_maps`, called from `update_materials`): each map is uploaded once (all mips through one staging buffer, RGBA8, an `_SRGB` view for colour maps) into a free slot and keyed by its `Arc` address (the Arc is held); maps no object uses any more are released after `wait_idle`. `map_count()` for the self-test.
- **Shader:** `intersect` computes UVs and a per-triangle tangent from the UV derivatives (glTF's stored MikkTSpace tangents are not read) only for materials with flag 16, then samples the maps there (`sample_maps`): fewer registers are live than in the bounce loop. `Hit` carries `albedo`, `rough_metal` and `textured`; `apply_maps` copies them into the material after the emission is taken (emission stays flat). The bitangent is −dP/dv (glTF v runs down the image); checked on Khronos NormalTangentMirrorTest (all 15 spheres match the geometry column). Texture sets are box-mapped along the normal's dominant axis: static geometry in world units, objects in their own axes at world size (`CommittedObjectRayOrigin` scaled by the transform's column lengths). Masked materials (flag 8) are FORCE_NON_OPAQUE instances and `alpha_hole` tests the base alpha at LOD 0 in both any-hit loops (checked on Khronos AlphaBlendModeTest). Ray offsets use the interpolated normal `ng`, shading the normal-mapped `n`.
- **Mip level from ray cones:** `cone_width` / `cone_spread` start at one pixel (orthographic: a pixel's width, no spread), grow along each segment, and each rough bounce adds 0.5·α (specular) or 0.5 (diffuse) to the spread. The footprint at a hit is the width / |cos| (capped at 20×) × texture units per world unit (UV area over world area per triangle, or the box-mapping scale); level = log2(footprint × texels).
- **Two path-tracer variants** (build.rs and hotreload.rs list them): `trace` without map code and `trace_maps` with `-D TEXTURE_MAPS`; `Gpu::textured` (any material with flag 16) picks one per frame. The map code alone cost untextured scenes about 7% even when never run (registers), so they now run the variant without it.
- **Perf** (1440×900, 300 frames, median of alternating runs vs the item-6 snapshot): untextured scenes unchanged (classic −0.1%, gold dragon +0.3%); the chess set with its 33 maps +16% (it rendered flat averages before; +33% before mips and ray cones); the textured showroom +12%.
- **Assets:** `tools/fetch_assets.ps1` adds 6 ambientCG sets (2K JPG zips pinned by SHA-256, only Color / NormalGL / Roughness / Metalness extracted: Bricks097, Fabric062, Marble016, Metal032, Tiles074, WoodFloor041) and 2 Poly Haven sets (laminate_floor_02, rock_wall_08, with ARM maps) into `scenes/textures/<id>/` (gitignored, hard-linked by the snapshot script, now recursively). `tools/build_scenes.py` `textured(folder, scale)` reads the averages with PIL; `scenes/materials` has 4 scenes (showroom, brick Cornell box, rock garden, duck on wood).
- **Tests:** unit tests for a textured glTF quad written by `mesh::write_test_gltf` (UVs, maps, averages, cutoff, flag), mips and halving, map names and prefixes, a folder set with a DirectX normal map. Self-test 269–271: the quad uploads 2 maps; switching *Texture maps* off releases them. (272 is the Assets window.)

## Animation and timeline (track 8, 2026-10-31)

- **Data** (`anim.rs`, `Settings::animation`, skipped in files while default): `Animation { length, fps, shutter, tracks }`, `Track { path, keys }`, `Key { time, value: f64, interp }`, `Interp` Smooth (Fritsch–Carlson monotone cubic: never overshoots the keys, so keyed values stay inside `validate()`; zero slope at the ends) / Linear / Ease (smoothstep) / Hold, per segment from its key. A track's `path` is a JSON pointer without the leading slash into the serialized `Settings` ("objects/2/position/0"). Only floats animate (`is_f64` in the JSON: u32 / i32 / enums / strings / bools never), minus `NOT_ANIMATED` (scale, HDR levels, filter width, split, rotate speed, animation). `Animation::apply(s, t)` serializes, writes each track's value, deserializes and validates (on failure `s` comes back unchanged; missing paths are skipped). `removed(list, index)` drops and renumbers tracks when `delete_selected` removes an object or light. `validate()` bounds length 0.1–3600 s, fps 1–240, shutter 0–1, sorted finite keys. `animation` is out of `transport_key` (its effect arrives through the fields).
- **Playhead** (`anim::Timeline` in `App::timeline`, not saved): `advance` while playing (loops or stops), `update` applies the animation only when the time or the keys changed since the last call, so an edit at a still playhead stays until it moves (as in Blender) and `rest` (the settings as last applied) tells what was edited. Run in `redraw` after egui, skipped during an export.
- **Keying:** `key_selection` (I) keys, under the selection's `prefixes` (object / light subtree, or the camera's or main light's top-level fields from `entity`), every float that already has a track or differs from `rest`, and on the entity's first key its transform list; `key_changed` (Shift+I) keys every float that differs from `rest`; Alt+I `remove_keys`. Each is an undo checkpoint and resets `rest` / `applied` to the keyed state.
- **UI** (`timeline_ui.rs`): `timeline_panel` (bottom panel above the status bar while `UiState::show_timeline` or the scene has keys; top-bar *Timeline*): |◀, previous key, Play (K), next key, ▶|, time, frame, Loop, Key, Delete key, interpolation of the selection's keys at the playhead, then length, fps, shutter (as an angle) and Clear; `track_strip` paints seconds, every key faint, the selection's keys in the accent colour and the playhead, and scrubs (snapped to frames unless Shift). Shift+Left / Right step a frame. `about_to_wait` keeps polling while playing.
- **Export** (`video::Options::timeline`, dialog *Motion → Timeline*, enabled when there are keys): `Job::start` takes fps and frames from the animation (`Animation::frames`), keeps the camera (no orbit overrides), stores `base` (the settings before animation) and the GPU scene's `geometry` / `materials` keys; `animate(g, t)` applies the keys and rebuilds the BLAS / TLAS or materials when they changed. Frame i is t = i / fps. **Motion blur** (shutter > 0): `before_render` (called before every pass, also in `video::render`) sets `g.painting` (no reset on scene changes, no RR) and moves to t_i + (u − 0.5)·shutter / fps with u stepped by the golden ratio per pass; `after_render` turns painting off before the read-back. `finish` turns painting off and rebuilds the user's scene when the job changed it (`scene_changed`). RR is therefore off for motion-blurred exports; orbit exports are unchanged.
- **Showcase:** `scenes/builtin/pool-break-1984.json` (`pool_break()` in build_scenes.py, with a `track()` helper): the cue strikes, the cue ball runs into the rack, three balls scatter; 1 s at 24 fps, 180° shutter.
- **Tests:** `anim::tests` (interpolations and no overshoot, apply skips ints / missing paths / invalid results, keying and index fixups). ## Diffraction grating (track 11 part 4, 2026-11-01)

- **Model** (`pathtrace.hlsl`, after `spec_probability`): grooves along the brush direction T (`aniso_frame`, so `anisotropy_rotation` and *Spun* place them: circles for a CD); order o of wavelength λ leaves where the tangential part of l is the mirror direction's moved by o·λ/spacing along B. Each order is the base GGX lobe (isotropic: an anisotropic grating keeps its anisotropy in the mirror part only) translated on the projected disk: f_o(l) = f(l0), with l0 = l moved back (`grating_move`), because f·cos·dω is f times projected area, which the move keeps. Orders ±1 take 0.35 of the strength each, ±2 0.15; an order whose mirror direction would leave past the horizon (evanescent; depends on v and λ only, `order_shares`) gives its share back to the mirror reflection, so a Blu-ray reflects plainly except at grazing views.
- **Sampling:** in the specular branch a grating material first picks an order by those shares, then moves an isotropic GGX sample by it; pdf_o(l) = pdf(l0)·cos l / cos l0, so an order sample weighs what a mirror sample does. `eval_bsdf` mixes the mirror's and the orders' pdfs, so MIS stays exact. Before this, orders reached by next-event estimation only: in a furnace (no lights) the grating spheres were dark and full of fireflies.
- **Wavelength:** one per grating hit (`grating_wavelength`, from a hash of pixel, sequence index and bounce, not the sampler's dimensions, so other materials keep their sequences), shared by that hit's NEE and BSDF sample. Importance-sampled from three Gaussian channel curves of equal area (R 600 ± 40, G 545 ± 35, B 455 ± 25 nm; a flat spectrum is white), so the RGB weight is 3·N_c / ΣN, at most 3. One wavelength per path multiplied the same weight in at every grating bounce: biased (E[w²] > 1) and fireflies of 1e7 between grating spheres. A float golden-ratio sequence (`sequence_index · 0.618`) had no fraction left at those magnitudes: every pixel drew 380 nm.
- **Data:** `Material::{grating (0–1), grating_spacing (µm, 0.3–10, default 1.6)}`, packed into `optical.w` as −(strength in thousandths × 16384 + spacing in nm) (`lighting::grating_code`): lights keep 0 / 1 there for two- / one-sided emission and the shader treats negative as two-sided, so the record does not grow. UI: *Diffraction grating*, *Groove spacing*; *Brush direction* and *Spun* show for gratings too. Not in glTF (no extension).
- **Variant:** grating code compiles only into `trace_maps` (`has_grating` = `texture_maps` && optical.w < 0), which `Gpu` now picks when any material has maps or a grating (`textured`). In `trace` it cost scenes without a grating 2–15% even when never run: two inlined `eval_bsdf` copies with four order lobes each. Tried and dropped: a rolled order loop (worse), one `eval_bsdf` copy shared by NEE and the BSDF sample in a two-pass loop (worse), per-hit precompute kept in the material copy (kept 8 more values live, no gain), a slimmer order body (helped part).
- **Checks** (debug, raw native): white furnace, 12 white-metal grating spheres (spacing 1.6 / 0.74 / 0.32 µm, roughness 0.05–0.5, spun and straight): 0.87–1.04 per channel, 9 pixels above 2 (max 11.7); residual colour at the rims: a rough order lobe partly past the horizon loses that part (red, shifted farthest, most), and the compensation tuned for the mirror lobe over-boosts an order moved inward (one blue patch 1.16). `optical-discs`, light-sampling-only vs MIS: 9×9-blurred RMSE×1000 1.86, off-disc pixels agree (mean −1e-5); on the discs light-only is 3.1% darker, the same scene without gratings 5.1% (the known offset on sharp metal, roadmap Known issues).
- **Scene:** `optical-discs` (`optical_discs()` and the `grating()` helper in build_scenes.py): a CD, a gold DVD and a Blu-ray lying on a dark stage and a CD standing up, under small lamps and a soft fill, so each shows rainbow streaks from the lamps and fans from the fill.
- **Cost** (release, 1440×900, 300 frames, two alternating runs against a release build of c4be8f1, GPU ms, RR / native): veach-mis 4.56 → 4.50 / 4.25 → 4.41, tea-set 4.84 → 4.77 / 5.65 → 5.65, layered-materials 4.49 → 4.52 / 4.87 → 4.91, glass-stack 6.29 → 6.22 / 6.91 → 7.08, brushed-metal 6.22 → 6.18 / 7.60 → 8.03 (within noise but brushed-metal native, about ±4% between runs); texture-brick-box (the maps variant, now with the grating code) 5.42 → 5.75 / 5.23 → 5.59; `optical-discs` 5.71 / 7.10 (4.54 / 5.37 with the gratings removed).

## Thin film (track 11 part 3, 2026-11-01)

- **Model** (glTF's KHR_materials_iridescence; `pathtrace.hlsl` `film_fresnel`, `spec_fresnel`): Belcour and Barla (2017), the Airy sum of a film between the outside and the base kept to two orders, with the spectral integral in closed form against Gaussian fits of the CIE matching functions (the Khronos sample viewer's constants), then XYZ → linear Rec. 709. No spectral rendering. IORs are relative to the outside: the film's, and the base's per channel from f0 ((1 + √f0) / (1 − √f0), so a metal's colour stands in for its complex IOR). Below 30 nm the film's IOR fades to the outside's (smoothstep), leaving the plain base Fresnel. It is evaluated once per hit at the view cosine, as glTF's model does (`prepare_film`, kept in the hit's copy of the material as `base_scale.rgb`), not per half vector: inlined at every Fresnel site it cost scenes without a film 3–9% in registers. The result replaces the base specular's Schlick term, lerped by the strength: the specular, the diffuse's 1 − F and the guides' specular albedo (RR and native AA) all see it. The coat and sheen are unchanged.
- **Dielectrics:** in the transmission branch the film's reflectance (outside = the medium the ray comes from, base = the one it enters, from the nested stack) replaces the exact Fresnel by the strength (not at total internal reflection): reflection is chosen by its mean over RGB and the throughput weighted by F / mean or (1 − F) / (1 − mean), so a transmissive sphere of IOR 1 with a 1.33 film is a soap bubble. The film adds no refraction (parallel walls).
- **Data:** `Material::{iridescence (0–1), iridescence_thickness (nm, 0–2000, default 400), iridescence_ior (1–2.5, default 1.3)}`, skipped in files while default. Packed into the free `surface_scale.zw` (no wider record): z = strength, w = thickness (whole nm) + 2048 × IOR in hundredths (`lighting::film_code`), exact as an integer float; `Gpu::attach_maps` now writes only `surface_scale.xy`. UI: *Thin film*, *Film thickness*, *Film IOR* in the advanced material rows. glTF: factor, IOR and `iridescenceThicknessMaximum` (the thickness texture is not read, so the maximum is used).
- **Energy compensation** with a film scales by the film's reflectance at normal incidence instead of f0 (`fc`).
- **Checks** (debug, raw native, `captures/film/`): white furnace, film over white metal at roughness 0 / 0.4 / 0.8 and thicknesses 200–700 nm: 0.98–1.00 of the background, none above it beyond the usual sphere-floor contact pixels; the soap bubble and film-coated glass vanish (1.00). A white diffuse base under a 450 nm film reads 0.915 / 0.959 / 0.924 (plain white diffuse 0.958): the diffuse is weighted by 1 − F(vh) while a rough GGX lobe returns less than F, which the base's 4% hides and a strong film shows (0.89 with f0 in the compensation, 0.90 with the per-half-vector film). `thin-film`, light-sampling-only vs MIS (320×200, 4096 spp): RMSE×1000 9.2 (the bubbles' delta paths), 9×9-blurred 1.25, mean −0.0003.
- **Scene:** `thin-film` (`thin_film()` and the `film()` helper in build_scenes.py): three soap bubbles (380 / 520 / 700 nm), anodized titanium at 60–260 nm oxide (IOR 2.4: blue, silver, bronze-magenta, cyan, gold), an oil slick (1.47, 420 nm) on a dark puddle and a beetle-green metal pawn.
- **Cost:** release, 1440×900, 300 frames, two alternating runs against a release build of `main` without the change, GPU ms, RR / native: veach-mis 4.06 → 4.24 / 3.90 → 4.07, tea-set 4.45 → 4.42 / 5.04 → 5.24, layered-materials 3.99 → 4.06 / 4.32 → 4.50, glass-stack 5.17 → 5.38 / 5.84 → 6.17 (about 1–5%, glass-stack 4–6%; runs differ by about 3%). `thin-film` itself 4.77 RR / 5.12 native. ## Energy compensation and anisotropy (track 11 part 2, 2026-10-31)

- **Energy compensation** (Turquin 2019, the one-table form of Kulla and Conty 2017; `eval_bsdf`): GGX with separable Smith G1·G1 keeps only E(nv, roughness) of the light (F = 1); a white metal at roughness 1 kept 0.31 of a furnace background. The base specular is multiplied by 1 + f0·(1/E − 1) (per channel; a white metal gains 1/E, a dielectric's 4% specular almost nothing and its diffuse keeps the rest). The coat lobe is compensated in full (1/E), and the layers below it are dimmed by `coat_albedo(nv, roughness)`, the compensated coat's real albedo with f0 0.04 (= the old coat_fresnel(nv) when smooth): F(nv) nears 1 at grazing, where a rough coat's facets mostly see far smaller F(vh), which left dark rims. Non-reciprocal (view-side factor), as Filament's; sampling is unchanged, so MIS stays consistent.
- **Tables** (`tools/fit_layers.py` → `shaders/layers.hlsli`, constant arrays, about 35 KB of source): `GGX_ALBEDO` and `COAT_ALBEDO` 32 × 32 (roughness × nv, bilinear), `ANISO_ALBEDO` 12 × 4 × 8 × 6 (nv × view azimuth from the brushing direction folded to 0–90° × roughness × strength, quadrilinear, 16 taps, read only by anisotropic materials). Each entry is importance-sampled on a stratified grid; checked against brute-force hemisphere integration to 5 digits. No single effective roughness fits the anisotropic albedo to the isotropic table: it varies by 0.53–0.66 with the view azimuth alone, and the geometric mean, RMS and other means left compensated albedos between 0.54 and 1.11.
- **Anisotropy** (glTF's KHR_materials_anisotropy model): `Material::{anisotropy (0–1), anisotropy_rotation (degrees, −180..180), anisotropy_spun}` → `layers.w`, `sheen.w` (radians), flag bit 11. The base GGX takes alpha along the brushing direction T and lerp(alpha, 1, strength²) across it, so highlights streak across the brushing. T comes from `intersect` (`Hit::tangent`, filled only when `layers.w > 0`): the object's x axis (world x for the room and floor), or with *Spun* `cross(object y, p − object origin)`, circles around the object's y axis (radial highlights on a disc, a pan or a sphere's meridians); `aniso_frame` projects it into the tangent plane, falls back to `basis` where it is parallel to the normal (the poles), and turns it by the rotation. The coat and sheen stay isotropic. UI: *Anisotropy*, *Brush direction*, *Spun* in the advanced material rows. glTF: strength and rotation are read; glTF's direction is the rougher axis, so import adds 90°, and the rotation turns from the object's x axis, not the UV tangent.
- **Sampling:** anisotropic materials sample visible normals (Heitz 2018, `ggx_sample_aniso`; spec pdf G1(v)·D/(4 nv)); D·nh sampling, kept for isotropic materials (same random numbers as before), put fireflies on the steep facets of a strongly anisotropic metal (spun sphere alone in the furnace at 4096 spp: pixels above 1.05, 42 → 22; isotropic twin: 4).
- **Checks** (debug, raw native, 2048–8192 spp): white furnace, the metal spheres at roughness 0.5 / 0.75 / 1 read 0.90 / 0.61 / 0.31 of the background before and 0.99–1.00 after; the layered furnace's coated metal 0.90 → 0.99, its rough coats' rims gone, the brushed and spun white metal spheres 0.99 (0.60 with a geometric-mean roughness lookup); no sphere above 1 beyond noise; rare bright pixels remain where a metal sphere meets the floor (a 5.2 one also with the isotropic coated metal). `brushed-metal`, light sampling only vs MIS: RMSE×1000 0.97, 9×9-blurred 0.11, mean ratio 1.0000; BSDF only 1.0010 (the known BSDF-only offset).
- **Scenes:** `brushed-metal` (spun steel disc with a gold ring, spun sphere, copper pan with a brushed handle, brushed aluminium plate and sphere, a polished sphere for comparison); `layered-furnace` gained brushed and spun white metal spheres.
- **Cost** (GPU ms, 1440×900, debug exe vs the track 11 part 1 release snapshot, interleaved, two runs each, RR / native): veach-mis 4.52 → 4.51 / 4.23 → 4.39, tea-set 4.64 → 4.77 / 5.28 → 5.68, gem-display 5.01 → 5.30 / 5.64 → 5.99, glass-stack 5.72 → 6.09 / 6.11 → 6.52, cornell-caustic 5.74 → 6.08 / 5.86 → 6.19, layered-materials 4.35 → 4.43 / 4.67 → 4.89; brushed-metal 6.2 / 7.9. Part of it is longer paths: kept energy keeps Russian roulette from ending them. The first version cost 5–10% more on scenes that never take the anisotropic branch: eval_bsdf was inlined four times (three light-sampling strategies and the BSDF sample) and its anisotropic code weighed on all of them; next-event estimation now picks ld / le / pl / limit per strategy and evaluates once (renders match to float reordering, max abs diff 1e-6). ## Layered materials: clear coat and sheen (track 11, 2026-10-31)

- **Model** (glTF's KHR_materials_clearcoat and _sheen; `pathtrace.hlsl` `eval_bsdf`): base (Lambert + GGX as before) → sheen over it → coat over both. Sheen: Charlie distribution (Estevez and Kulla 2017, α = roughness², roughness clamped to 0.07) with their fitted Λ visibility, times `sheen.rgb`, divided by max(1, E(nv)) (the lobe's albedo passes 1 only at grazing views below roughness ~0.2); the base is dimmed by 1 − max(sheen)·E(nv). Coat: GGX with its own roughness, Schlick f0 0.04 (IOR 1.5), times `layers.x`; the base is dimmed by 1 − cc·F(nv). Both dim by the view direction only (glTF's form): dimming by both directions (reciprocal) lost 8–12% of a white base in the furnace. NEE and BSDF sampling evaluate the same function, so MIS stays consistent. Layers apply to the opaque part only (scaled by 1 − transmission, as the base).
- **Sampling:** the coat's own GGX lobe with probability `coat_probability` = 0.3·cc, otherwise the old spec / diffuse choice; the sheen rides on the diffuse lobe's cosine directions (no pdf term). The coat's random number is drawn only when cc > 0, so other materials keep their sample sequences (bit-identical). `ggx_sample` is the old inline GGX sampling, factored out.
- **Table:** `shaders/layers.hlsli` (generated by `tools/fit_layers.py`, 16 × 16, rows roughness 0..1, columns nv 0..1, bilinear `sheen_albedo`) holds the sheen's directional albedo as a constant array: no GPU resource. Neubelt's visibility was tried first and reaches an albedo of 5.7 at grazing.
- **Data:** `Material::{clearcoat, clearcoat_roughness (0.03), sheen, sheen_color (white), sheen_roughness (0.5)}`, all 0–1, skipped in files while default; `PackedMaterial::layers` = (coat, coat roughness, sheen roughness, −), `sheen` = colour × strength (both appended: 144 B per material). ## Nested dielectrics (track 10, 2026-10-31)

- **Rule** (Schmidt and Budge 2002, `pathtrace.hlsl` `medium_*`): each path keeps a stack of the transmissive materials it is inside: `uint64_t media`, four 16-bit entries from the bottom, `(material id + 1) | priority << 13`, 0 = empty; a full stack drops the push; ids above 8190 would alias (objects end at 4100). The current medium is the highest `Material::priority` (0–7, flag bits 8–10 of `lobes.w`, packed in `lighting::pack`), the latest entry on ties; empty = air (IOR 1). A surface with `transmission > 0` is a **false hit** when the ray enters it below the current medium's priority, or leaves a material that is in the stack but is not the current medium: the stack is updated, the ray continues from `p − ng·0.0003` in the same direction, no bounce is counted (`false_hits` ≤ 32 per path) and `previous_pdf` / `previous_delta` / `previous_position` stay, so MIS still refers to the last real vertex.
- **Cost:** the skip runs in a small loop around `intersect` at the top of the bounce loop (absorption and cone width per skipped segment there), not as a `continue` of the bounce loop. Measured on cornell-caustic at 1440×900 (debug exe, GPU time; HEAD 5.34 ms RR): a `uint4` stack plus a `continue` block cost 6.2 ms, the 64-bit word 5.8, the inner loop 5.4. Run-to-run noise is about ±0.3 ms, so measure each variant twice and against a same-session baseline. A true hit refracts with n1 / n2 taken from the stack (entering: the current medium → this one; leaving: this one → what the stack falls back to). Leaving a material that is not in the stack (the camera started inside it) keeps the old rule: into the current medium, or air.
- **Absorption** follows the current medium (`medium_absorption`, from the material's flat colour, so patterns and maps no longer tint it) and is recomputed only when a refraction changes the stack; false hits leave it, so a segment through a false surface keeps the medium it travels in.
- **Fresnel:** dielectrics now use the exact unpolarised Fresnel (`fresnel_dielectric`) instead of Schlick, which was poor between two dense media and reflected at grazing angles at n1 = n2. The TIR test is part of it (F = 1), and the reflect / refract choice always draws one random number (Schlick's path skipped it on TIR). Old glass scenes change slightly (see the roadmap entry). The guides' `specular_albedo` still uses Schlick.
- **Defaults:** every material has priority 0 (`skip_serializing_if` zero, so old scene files are unchanged); with equal priorities the latest entered medium wins, which makes touching or overlapping glass and water meet without an air gap. Ranking matters only for overlaps: in `glass-of-water` the water reaches into the glass wall and base (water 1 < glass 2), the ice pokes out of the water (3) and the air bubbles (IOR 1.0, 4) sit inside it.
- **Mesh:** `gen:glass/<seed>` (`procedural::glass`) is two lathed parts sharing one normalisation, *Glass* (chamfered foot, rounded rim, filleted inner floor; 0.045 wall, 0.2 base at height 2) and *Water* (side and floor 0.4 wall inside the glass, a meniscus rising 0.03 at the wall); seeds vary radius (0.55–0.65), taper (0–0.15) and fill (55–80%). `procedural::lathe` revolves an outline in (r, y) with the solid on its left, (dy, −dx) as the normal and smooth points averaging their edges; a band on the axis is a fan of single triangles. Test `glass_and_water_are_outward_and_nested` checks the volume by the divergence theorem (positive only with outward normals) and that the water stays below the rim.
- **UI:** *Priority* (0–7) in the material's advanced rows (`editor::material_ui`); `validate()` bounds it.
- **Priority as CSG** (scenes `layered-cocktail`, `paperweights`, `aquarium`, `perfume-vanity`; helpers `ranked`, `clear`, `cyl`, `block`, `ball` in build_scenes.py): a solid glass cylinder or box (1) with a liquid (2–4) and an air shape (IOR 1.0, 5–6) inside it reads as a filled vessel with no inner wall to model; a liquid or air shape that rises past the rim opens the glass's top (the rim is a false hit inside it), and one kept inside seals it. Overlap neighbouring liquids by about 0.01 rather than letting faces coincide. Anything that must poke out of the liquid into the air (ice) needs the top priority (7). Opaque objects (fish, gravel, coins) simply sit inside a medium. Pitfall: a flat-bottomed solid resting on the floor shows the floor under it fully lit (shadow rays pass glass) plus caustic fireflies, and its bottom is coplanar with the floor. It stays small under the cocktail glasses, the block paperweight and the bottles, but dominated a wide solid disc, so the millefiori paperweight is a low dome resting on a curve. Cost (debug exe, 1440×900, GPU ms, RR / native): layered-cocktail 9.4 / 11.3, aquarium 8.8 / 9.7, perfume-vanity 7.5 / 9.0, paperweights 6.8 / 7.9, against glass-of-water 10.6 / 12.6; layer-sweeps 4.8 / 5.7, velvet-lounge 6.2 / 7.6, layered-furnace 3.6 / 4.1.
- **Checks** (debug build, 400², raw native, 4096 spp, against a 16k-spp reference; `captures/nested/`): the water tank with an inner box of the same IOR and colour ranked above the water matches the plain tank to noise (RMSE×1000 7.76 vs 6.34; the plain render shares its first 4096 samples with the reference, which explains the 1.2×); an IOR-1.5 orange box ranked *below* the water vanishes (6.42), while the same box at equal priority shows (23.4).

## Instancing (core track 4, 2026-10-30)

- **Layout:** `Gpu::rebuild_scene` builds (1) the static BLAS from `geometry::static_geometry` (floor, room, main light, area lights; world space, split into non-opaque / opaque ranges as before, `opaque_offset` in `environment.w`), only when its vertices change; (2) one BLAS per distinct mesh key (`geometry::object_source`: `shape:<Kind>` or the `mesh` string), object-space, in `meshes`; (3) a TLAS of the static instance (InstanceID 0) and one instance per visible object (`geometry::object_transform`, DXR 3×4 row-major).
- **Mesh buffer:** every mesh in use is packed into one object-space vertex buffer (t1). When the set of mesh keys changes it is rebuilt and only new keys get a BLAS (a built BLAS holds its own copy of the triangles, so old ones are reused with new `first` offsets). The static vertices live in their own buffer (t6), so light edits don't touch the meshes.
- **Instance table** (t7, `InstanceRecord` ↔ `Instance` in common.hlsli): `vertex_base`, `material` (5 + object index; u32::MAX for the static instance, whose vertices carry ids), `emitter_base` (start of this instance's range in `emitter_lookup`, u32::MAX for non-emissive objects), `object`. Built in `rebuild_scene`, emitter bases filled and uploaded in `update_materials`.
- **Emitters** stay world-space triangles: static ones, then each emissive object's `object_geometry` (in instance order). The self-test's count check (full soup vs GPU) still holds.
- **Shader:** `candidate_material(instance, prim)` in both `Proceed()` loops; `intersect` reads the instance, then static vertices (+ `opaque_offset` for geometry 1) or `vertices[vertex_base + prim·3]` with the normal taken to world space as `mul(n, (float3x3)CommittedWorldToObject3x4())` (inverse transpose).
- `geometry::lowest_y` caches grounding per (mesh pointer, rotation, scale) because instancing asks for every object's frame on every rebuild (a chess part scans the whole 1.6M-vertex file).
- `frame_objects` is exact up to 256 objects; past that it uses each mesh's box corners through the instance transform (a 3600-object forest took minutes in the debug unit tests). The generator test runs 3 seeds for forest / crowd; unit tests take about 60 s in debug.
- `MAX_OBJECTS` = 4096. New generator families `forest` (400–1800 trees from 6 shared tree meshes, two objects each) and `crowd` (up to 54² copies of one shape).
- **Measured** (1440×900, release, median of 3 alternating runs vs the core3 snapshot): drag frame time bunny 26 → 5.5 ms, chess preset 24 → 6.1 ms, ASWF chess set 141 → 7 ms. Plain render GPU time: Classic +7% RR / +5% native, bunny +1 / +5%, chess +6 / +2%, mirror corridor −3 / −3%, neon −2 / +1% (two-level traversal costs a little in tiny scenes).
- **Render check** vs the core3 build (raw native, 512 and 4096 spp): RMSE ×1000 below 0.2 on presets 0, 2, 8, 13, 20 and the chess set; glass scenes differ by noise (the glass dragon 7.4 → 2.7 from 512 to 4096 spp). The glass lab (14) keeps a difference where glass objects' bottom faces lie exactly on the floor: coplanar faces z-fight, and the instance transform moves them by float rounding, so the other face wins in places. Neither build is more correct.

## HDRI environments (core track 3, 2026-10-30)

- **Settings:** `hdri` (path, ≤ 1024, relative to `scenes/` like meshes), `hdri_strength` (0–1000), `hdri_rotation` (±360°), `hdri_visible` (camera rays see it; off, bounce-0 misses return `background`). - **GPU:** `Gpu::set_environment` uploads both through `upload_texture` (staging per footprint row) into textures that live across resizes (`env_views` at heap slots 30, 31, re-created in `resize`). NEE picks the HDRI with `p_env` = 0.5 when emissive triangles exist (1 otherwise); triangle NEE pdfs and BSDF-hit weights carry (1 − p_env). Misses after a non-delta bounce get the MIS weight against `p_env·env_pdf`. The light-sampling modes (`mis_mode`) work as for triangles. ## Toys (roadmap 5d, 2026-10-30)

## Camera and look (roadmap 5c, 2026-10-30)

- **Constants grew past 512 B:** passes now sit at `CB_STRIDE` = 1024 (trace 0, filter 1–7, display 8; buffer 9 KB, compile-time assert). New blocks: `lens`, `camera_model`, `look`, `lift` (w = grain cell size), `grade_gamma` (w = LUT strength), `gain` (w = frame seed). Mirror any change in common.hlsli.
- **Trace:** `camera_ray(pixel, lens_sample, …)` handles every model. Guides use the pinhole ray (`lens_sample` false), so RR and the denoiser guides stay sharp.
  - Lens: `aperture_point()` gives a disk or an n-gon fan. The focal plane is at `focus_distance` along the view axis, or tilted about the camera right axis (model 4).
  - Cat-eye rejects lens points outside a circle shifted by the pixel's NDC (`valid=false` → zero throughput).
  - Ortho uses `camera_model.w` = distance·tan(fov/2) as the half height. Equirect is ±π by ±π/2. The little planet is a stereographic projection looking down.
  - Non-perspective guide depth is the hit distance.
- **`Settings::perspective()`** (models 0 and 4) gates RR (`rr_active`), the temporal upscalers (`prepare_upscaler`), click picking and the gizmo.
- **Display:**
  - `lens_and_film` adds halation and vignette on linear light before the tone map. Halation is 24 taps of `scene_at` (RR/SR output or the native image) above 1.0, tinted red-orange.
  - `film_look(t)` does lift/gamma/gain on the encoded value, then the LUT (manual trilinear over u29). It runs inside `tonemap` and in `hdr_map`'s tone-mapped branch (not the Linear HDR branch).

  - Tone maps 6 AgX and 7 PBR Neutral (`validate` allows < 8).
- **LUT:** u29 is a 3D RGBA32F texture (`texture_3d`) kept outside `textures`, so `resize` only re-creates its view (`lut_view`, heap slot 29). The root UAV range is `TEXTURES + 1`. `look::read_cube` parses .cube (3D, domain 0..1, size 2–128). `App` reloads it when `lut_path` changes.
- **Probe** is now `gpu::Probe { linear, albedo, depth, id }` (depth from u5, id from u19). Ctrl+left-click sets `focus_pending`; the next readback's depth becomes `focus_target`, eased with 1 − e^(−10·dt) per frame.
